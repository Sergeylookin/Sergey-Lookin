01 / 11 → Symptoms

Sound familiar?

The signs that design has
stopped working for the business.

  1. 01

    Marketing, product and engineering pull in different directions.

    Blurs focus.
    Slows launches.
  2. 02

    Every launch is built from scratch — past projects don’t speed up the next.

    Effort doesn’t become speed.
  3. 03

    The team is busy, but the system gets no stronger.

    Knowledge doesn’t become a system.
  4. 04

    The brand drifts across channels. The look depends on whoever picked it up.

    Recognition drops, trust fades.
  5. 05

    Leadership is buried in review rounds. There’s less and less room for strategy.

    Strategy keeps slipping.

Chaos rarely looks like chaos.
More often, it looks like
a temporary fix that quietly
stayed forever.

There’s no single button
for this. No quick mockup, either.

The longest argument
of my career.
02 / 11 → Approach

A co-author, not a contractor.

Design enters the task before the brief is written.
Otherwise it fixes symptoms, not the problem.

Service
Receives a brief,
returns a mockup
  • Gets the brief already framed
  • Asks how, not why
  • Solves whatever lands on the desk
  • Sits at the end of the pipeline
Partner
Shapes the decision
from idea to launch
  • Helps frame the brief itself
  • Talks directly to marketing, product, leadership
  • Changes the what, not just the how
  • Sits at the start of the pipeline

Design is a co-author
of the business. A voice
at the table where
decisions are made.

What doesn’t fit
in a portfolio.
03 / 11 → Principles

What I stand on.

Four statements that hold up the rest.

01
Form doesn’t save
a weak idea

The problem, context and answer come first. The visual language comes after — to make it precise and visible.

02
A system works
without its author

Rules, components and logic should hold up under new briefs, new people and new channels.

03
Beautiful and effective —
not two requirements, but one.

Design has to earn trust, speed up decisions, lift conversion and build recognition.

04
The team understands
not just what, but why

The team has to understand the criteria, the language, the constraints — and why each decision was made.

It’s not pictures
that create the impression.
It’s design.

05 / 11 → Brand strategy

How a brand takes shape.

A brand isn’t a logo. It’s repeated decisions: how we sound, how we look, how we move, how we hold the bar.

Context

Market, competitors, audience, business goals. What we know — and what we’re only guessing.

Position

Who we’re for, who we’re against, and what we deliberately stay silent on. Without the second, the first doesn’t work.

Voice

How the brand reads in an email, on a banner, in support. The tone people recognise.

Visual language

Typography, colour, imagery, motion, photography. Not just a logo and palette — a whole grammar.

System

How the identity scales into web, social, product, decks, OOH.

Launch

Measurement, iteration, share of voice, brand recall. A brand isn’t a launch. It’s upkeep.

Work is done
when it works

06 / 11 → Evaluation

How I evaluate the work.

Four levels. A solution only passes
if it works on all four.

Level 1
Logic
The user gets it: where to tap, what happens, why it matters. If it needs explaining, the solution is weak.
Got it?
Level 2
Form
Hierarchy, contrast, rhythm, typography, balance. This is where craft lives — and where expression earns its place.
Crafted?
Level 3
System
Will it scale to ten channels? Hold up under new scenarios? Stay aligned with the brand?
Holds?
Level 4
Business
Does it help the business? Conversion, retention, recognition, speed.
Worked?

09 / 11 → Craft

Not a science. Not an art.

What people feel — and cannot put into words.

01 One brand red.

Со стороныOne brand red.

Thousands of shades of one red. Warm reads as trust. Cool reads as alarm. Light disappears, dark crushes. Without context and a pair beside it, colour means nothing.

02 One button on the screen.

Со стороныOne button on the screen.

Nine states. Eight aren’t in the screenshot. A button lives in motion — not in the mockup. A good one answers. A bad one doesn’t.

03 One typeface.

Со стороныOne typeface.

Eighteen weights — for body, headings, numbers, quotes. Size raises the voice. Typography has its own laws. Reading is ours alone.

04 One composition on the page.

Со стороныOne composition on the page.

Grid. Hierarchy. Rhythm. The air between blocks — also an element. What the eye reads before the word, and reacts to before the mind.

05 The task of drawing a mockup.

Со стороныThe task of drawing a mockup.

Pulling colour, button, type and rhythm — into one feeling. So a person looks and wants, without knowing why. And couldn’t describe it if they tried.

Everyone talks about design.
No one really knows what it is.

Strong design
holds up in reality.
10 / 11 → Results

What the business gets.

What shifts inside the team — and in how the product feels.

01
BRAND

One brand across every touchpoint

Landing, product, ads, deck — pieces of one system. The customer sees maturity, not five different companies.

02
SPEED

Releases ship several times faster

When the team works from a system, design stops being the bottleneck. What took a month ships in a week.

03
FOCUS

Teams moving the same way

Marketing, product, engineering lean on the same principles. Less politics — more work for the market.

04
TRUST

The brand reads more expensive

When touchpoints are coherent, price feels earned. Premium isn’t gold trim. It’s order.

05
CRITERIA

Decisions by criteria, not by taste

Design stops being personal opinion. The argument shifts from “I don’t like it” to “this works.”
